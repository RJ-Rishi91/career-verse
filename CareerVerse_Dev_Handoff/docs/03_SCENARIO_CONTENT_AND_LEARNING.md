# Scenario Content and Learning Design

## 1. Scenario design standard

Every scenario must feel like a plausible workplace situation with a
real business objective. Avoid generic quiz framing. Choices should test
communication, judgment, role boundaries, and follow-through.

Each scenario needs: - Learning objectives. - Business objective. -
Scenario type and context. - Client profile and relevant history. -
Prerequisites and expected next state. - Dialogue/email text. - Decision
nodes with 2--4 options each. - Deterministic branch targets and
effects. - 7C and other scoring metadata. - Critical mistakes and
severity. - Relationship/account effects. - Coaching and explanation. -
Completion criteria and rewards. - Domain/accessibility review
metadata. - Content version and reviewer details.

## 2. Core scenario families

MVP should prioritize insurance workflows: - Quote creation and quote
status. - Policy issuance and renewals. - COIs (Certificates of
Insurance). - Policy documents and endorsements. - Claims coordination
and verification. - Documentation review. - Client/POC progress
updates. - Carrier communication. - Adjuster communication. - Insured
questions, renewals, premiums, and modifications, within the VA's
authority.

Later content can expand to other customer support, query resolution,
accounts, leadership, business development, and international markets.

## 3. Communication framework: 7Cs

1.  Clear --- understandable without unnecessary interpretation.
2.  Concise --- no avoidable wording.
3.  Concrete --- specific facts, actions, owners, and timelines rather
    than vague words.
4.  Correct --- accurate language, facts, terminology, and commitments.
5.  Coherent --- logical order and connected ideas.
6.  Complete --- includes the information or next step required.
7.  Courteous --- respectful, suitable tone.

Evaluate each C independently, then apply weighted scoring and
critical-failure rules. Multiple responses can earn partial credit where
reasonable.

## 4. Other learning dimensions

Score or coach: - Grammar/language control. - Tone and
professionalism. - Empathy/emotional intelligence. - Comprehension. -
Problem-solving and judgment. - Business effectiveness. - Client/account
outcome. - Role-specific competencies.

Keep language quality distinct from business effectiveness. Do not
reward fancy vocabulary when plain, accurate wording is better.

## 5. Coaching template

For each completed call: 1. What worked. 2. What could be improved. 3.
Why the choice mattered in this business context. 4. Relevant 7C or
skill. 5. A better alternative or practical rule. 6. Business/client
impact. 7. Recommended next action.

Tone must be constructive and non-shaming. Explain critical mistakes
clearly without humiliating the learner.

## 6. Emails and vocabulary

Email missions ask players to identify sender intent, extract
facts/deadlines/requests, notice missing information or risks, and
choose a response or next action. Emails remain part of the client's
history.

Vocabulary should be learned in context through calls, emails, a
personal glossary, recall challenges, examples, and later reuse.
Prioritize clear, useful workplace language over difficult words.

## 7. Client thoughts and emotional intelligence

Thoughts unlock progressively with trust, EI, and career skill. They
provide clues, not answer keys. All thoughts are authored and
deterministic.

Conflicts become more likely after Level 3. EI/problem-solving demands
increase after Level 4. Level 8 introduces creative/critical thinking
and important decisions.

## 8. Country and market context

Planned story sequence: US → Canada → UK → Austria. The US is the
primary initial context, Canada secondary. Country-specific training
should cover phrasing, terminology, etiquette, expectations, and
realistic exceptions without stereotypes. Provide short orientation
modules before market-specific missions.

## 9. Insurance accuracy and authority boundaries

Mandatory rules: - Never teach the player to commit beyond their role or
authority. - Do not present uncertain coverage, claim approval, policy
issuance, premium, or binding status as confirmed. - Teach verification,
clear statements of known facts, identification of missing information,
and appropriate escalation. - Distinguish a process update from a
binding or coverage decision. - Require review by an insurance
subject-matter reviewer before publishing domain-specific scenarios. -
Present scenarios as educational simulations, not legal, insurance, or
compliance advice.

## 10. Draft/review/publish workflow

Draft content is not playable. Before review, validate required fields,
option counts, graph links, prerequisites, scoring metadata, critical
mistakes, and learning objectives. Review domain accuracy, communication
quality, scoring fairness, accessibility, and authority boundaries.
Publish as an immutable version and log reviewer/publisher and time.
Retiring content must not silently change committed player history.

## 11. Content scope control

Use reusable templates and shared nodes to reduce implementation and QA
cost, but do not erase meaningful consequences. Every scenario should
have a clear purpose; avoid branch complexity that adds no learning or
narrative value.
